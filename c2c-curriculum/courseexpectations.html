Course Rules

  1. It all begins with a math diagnostic and discussion with student, educator and sometimes parents/guardians. Most people just want to get good enough marks and do not care at all about learning. One day Bravo Math will be able to help those students, but that day is not today (Oct 2026). Students who qualify for C2C will be placed into the lowest level course that they haven't mastered. If a 32-year-old adult ed student does 15 minus 9 on their fingers, that is where we start. Period.
  2. Students must attend two 1-hour lessons per week. They must arrive on time and prepared to learn. They must be respectful to everyone in the community. The teacher is responsible for teaching what that all means. Violating the rules can result in immediate suspension or expulsion without warning. Bravo Math will one day be able to help less mature students, but that day is not today (Oct 2026).
  3. Students must do daily homework, not merely to the point of completion, but to the point of mastery. They should be ready to nail any pop quiz on any content they have ever covered with us. And there will be many (diagnostic) quizzes.
  4. Each course will follow the same short-cycle schedule. The shortness improves feedback loops for everyone. Students do not need to dread repeating a course or waiting a year to rewrite a test. And Bravo Math will get better feedback and iterate its content seven times faster than a year-long math course.
    • Week 1: Lessons 1 and 2
    • Week 2: Lessons 3 and 4
    • Week 3: Lessons 5 and 6
    • Week 4: Lessons 7 and 8
    • Week 5: Lessons 9 and Transition Test, Attempt 1 (see below)
    • Week 6: Attempt 1 Feedback. Attempt 2 (see below)
  5. Bravo Math has a particular testing routine, all in an attempt to seek truthfulness and incentive alignment.
    • We normalize taking two attempts, which eliminates penalties for early struggles while rewarding learning from experience.
    • A transition test is the sole criterion for both certifying completion of one course and for entry into the next course. Each test is marked as either "Mastered" in which case the student will receive certification and move on or "Try Again" in which case the student must retake the course and/or rewrite the test at the next available time. There is no charge for second or third attempts. And don't worry if you get a "Try Again" - the course is only 6 weeks long!
    • To help counter grade inflation, "Mastered" must mean mastery. That means both the student and the teacher must predict, in probabilistic terms, how well the student will do in the next course as well as a justification for that prediction.
    • Each test will have two or three sections. The first section, which will not always be present, is "Cumulative" covering any content from any previous course. The second is "Automaticity" which covers routine exercises and basic reasoning. It is strictly time-limited. The third is a non-routine task, such as teaching the content, a twist on routine exercises, a more complex application, etc.

Course Content

Counting to Calculus (C2C) covers only "bottleneck content" to bridge those two points in a student's math journey. See Hypothesis 1.

Course Description
Integers 1: How Many? Counting forwards and backwards from 0 to 100, Not a priority as of Sept 2026.
Integers 2: Add & Subtract within 100 Problem structures, math facts, and mental math.
Integers 3: Multiply & Divide within 100 Problem structures, math facts, and mental math.
Integers 4: All Operations within 100 Multi-step problem structures and mental math.
Integers 5: Place Value Scaling Integers 1-4 by factors of 10 (e.g. Relating 9+7 to 900 million plus 700 million).
Integers 6: Negativity Extending the above to negative integers.
Fractions 1: Fractions are Numbers Counting, representing, and comparing fractions, all in relation to whole numbers.
Fractions 2: Whole & Fraction Arithmetic Problem structures and mental math.
Fractions 3: Fraction & Fraction Arithmetic Problem structures and mental math.
Fractions 4: Rational Numbers Extending Fractions 1-3 to decimals, percents, ratios, rates, proportions, and negatives.
Algebra 1: Solutions & Equality Solutions, Solving, and Preservation of Equality.
Algebra 2: Recursion & Linear Relations Generality and predictiveness of algebra. Rate of change. Representing relations with tables, graphs, equations, diagrams, stories, etc.
Algebra 3: Functions Extending Algebra 1 & 2 to quadratic and exponential relations.
Calculus Rate of change vs rate of accumulation, Fundamental Theorem of Calculus.
Statistics Probability distribution functions, causal inference.

Learning Rules

i.e. How to learn as much (math) as possible.

  1. Spend most of your effort on tasks barely beyond what you've already mastered... Even if that's not what you're doing in school.
  2. Test your output frequently.
  3. Get the incentives and inspiration right.
  4. Complete the loop: Relate, Retrieve, Re-Evaluate.
    • Relate. Think deeply! Ask yourself Tracy Zager's questions.
    • Retrieve. Recall from memory your deep thinking. Test yourself! Repeat as necessary. (See Rule 2 above.)
    • Re-Evaluate. How did you do? How do you know? What's next? How do you know?
  5. Know that You Can Grow Your Brain! (as long as you apply Rule 1).
  6. Organize Your Stuff.
  7. Organize Your time.

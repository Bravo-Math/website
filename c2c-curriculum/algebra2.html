Course Outline

Essential Questions

  1. When is recursion a good way to think about relationships? When are 2-variable relationships a better way? What are the strengths and weaknesses of each method?
  2. How do mathematicians represent the concept of change? What about rate of change?
  3. What makes a relation linear? How do mathematicians represent that linearity?
  4. How did knowing the definition of "solution" and "equation" help us in this unit?
  5. How does math help make digital art?
  6. The most famous saying in statistics: "Correlation isn't causation." What does this mean? Why do we need statisticians to tell us this?

    1. JUMP 8 Formulas, Tables, and Graphs

  4. Crack the code (formula) on even HARDER VisualPatterns.org .
    1. Intermediate: #12, 32, 436, 437

    1. JUMP 7.1 Solving by Inverse Operations

So I can...

  • Recognize situations in which recursion is tedious or inexact.

  • Understand letter variable as an abbreviation for a class of numbers rather than a fixed and known number.
